Epigenetic Consciousness

The body keeps a record of the conditions it lived inThe body keeps the recordExperience changes which genes are switched on, without changing the genes themselves. In rats, how much a mother licks and grooms her pups changes how their stress genes are read for life.Weaver et al. (2004), Epigenetic programming by maternal behavior, Nature NeuroscienceMoore (2015), The Developing Genome, Oxford University Press. History is the path that produced those conditionsHistory is the pathRacial gaps in health track the conditions people are made to live in, not their genes. Inequality becomes embodied.Kuzawa and Sweet (2009), Epigenetics and the embodiment of race, American Journal of Human Biology. Read together, they become a consciousness: the power to see what was written, and to write again.

One breeds into the next. You cannot have behavioral epigenetics without becoming epigenetically literate. Add the history, and you arrive at a consciousness that understands nature and nurture, and what the environment an organism lives in does to its biology.

Concept equations: a way to hold the idea, not arithmetic and not a molecular claim.

The harm is not passed down.Not passed downIn humans, these marks are not shown to travel across generations through egg and sperm. That is why the harm has to be done again: the persistence is social, not genetic.Heard and Martienssen (2014), Transgenerational epigenetic inheritance: myths and mechanisms, Cell It is done again.Done againRepeated stress from the same social conditions wears the body down over a life. Black women in the U.S. show it earlier: weathering.Geronimus (1992), The weathering hypothesis, Ethnicity & DiseaseMcEwen (1998), Protective and damaging effects of stress mediators, New England Journal of Medicine

Each generation, the social stamp takes a new form and does the same work on the body. Change the room, the law, the funding, and the next generation comes out unstamped. Harm and repair, one physics.
